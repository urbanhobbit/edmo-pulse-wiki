## [2026-10-15] New Issue
- New issue: **Ukraine strikes on Yandex and Russian trolls: facts, propaganda and doubts**
- Date: October 15, 2026
- URL: https://edmo.eu/publications/ukraine-strikes-on-yandex-and-russian-trolls-facts-propaganda-and-doubts/
- Total issues: 25
- Sections: ON THE RISE, ZOOM-IN, ELECTION BEAT, GLOBAL PULSE, ON A DIFFERENT NOTE (all 5)
- Note: The week's organising question — did Ukrainian drone strikes on Yandex data centres (8-11 October) actually cripple Russian bot/propaganda activity? ON THE RISE: a viral narrative claims that after Ukrainian strikes on several Yandex data centres, online disinformation (about Ukraine and other topics) declined significantly, along with engagement generated by pro-Russian and far-right propaganda in Europe — a story amplified by Zelensky and backed by some governments (including Poland's) and politicians because, if true, it would be tangible evidence that extremist anti-EU, pro-Russian popularity is artificially inflated by Moscow-run manipulation. As of 13 October experts still debate it: a chart purporting to show AfD-linked bot traffic down 56% was AI-generated; other analyses found no significant fluctuations in monitored Russian bot activity; still others found something did happen, though more nuanced than "Russian bots disappeared". EDMO notes the sheer number of users reporting a change in their everyday social-media experience (especially on X) means the issue cannot be dismissed as successful pro-Ukrainian propaganda, anti-Russian disinformation or collective autosuggestion, and reports that it polled fact-checkers across its network: some saw sudden drops in TikTok engagement on political hashtags tied to far-right/pro-Russian forces; some saw significant behavioural shifts but could not attribute causes (the strikes may have mattered, but so may other explanations); others recorded no decrease in pro-Russian activity. Verdict at this stage: no conclusive evidence that the attacks significantly reduced activity of accounts linked to Russian propaganda and disinformation. EDMO calls on national/European agencies holding conclusive data to publish it so independent organisations can verify it — otherwise political-institution statements risk reflecting their own agendas — and urges caution, warning against confirmation bias. ZOOM-IN: network organisations investigated the claimed decline of Russian bot activity after the three consecutive strikes (8-11 October); the drop in pro-Kremlin traffic remains contested and all reports ask for more evidence, while evidence keeps mounting of a well-organised, multilayered Kremlin-run influence infrastructure in Europe (YouTube influencer recruitment in Estonia, Russian cyberattacks in the Nordics, disinformation via bogus think tanks). BROD/Funky Citizens compared TikTok views on Romanian political hashtags 8-11 October vs 1-4 October: views fell for 54 of 82 hashtags — #turul2inapoi (-61.2%), #suveranisti (-81.2%), #georgesimionofficial (-95.1%) — while #georgesimion (+51.3%) and #sos (+19.7%) rose; but there is no robust evidence of an operational link between TikTok accounts, hashtags and Yandex infrastructure — temporal correlation, not proven causality. IBERIFIER/Maldita.es: experts warn against hasty conclusions; Cloudflare Radar shows the drop in bot-driven traffic from Yandex infrastructure is real but cannot prove an effect on the spread of Russian propaganda (much of it is legitimate search-engine crawlers), and some experts monitoring Russian influence ops saw no significant decrease — on 13 October there was not sufficient evidence the strikes "neutralised" Russian bots. NORDIS/TjekDet: no evidence the strikes cut far-right (especially AfD) coverage either — bot traffic includes legitimate indexing software, Common Consultancy found no significant engagement variation on AfD/MAGA Facebook profiles, and the provenance/methodology of the viral "drastic drop" graphic remain unknown. ELECTION BEAT: BECID/Re:Baltica on Latvia's 3 October vote — PM Andris Kulbergs' United List won a historic 41% of seats, a record for a single force, with populism rising, pro-Russia voices gaining and a campaign dominated by social media (daily video blogs, promises to cut bureaucracy and spending; Re:Baltica found Kulbergs' statements misleading/false more often than other politicians), though Latvia's pro-European foreign policy and Ukraine support should continue; far-right and pro-Kremlin parties gained ground — Latvia First (Ainārs Šlesers) 17 seats and pro-Russian Sovereign Power/Young Latvians Alliance (Jūlija Stepaņenko) 15 — exploiting fears over immigration, vaccination, environment and security, with Re:Baltica warning the new parliament will have more MPs unbothered by disinformation and pro-Russian views and will care less about human rights, NGOs and media freedom. IBERIFIER/Chequeado on Spain's snap general election called by Pedro Sánchez for 29 November after parliament rejected two housing decrees amid public anger over the housing crisis (after a summer dominated by the Ceuta crisis); both chambers of the Cortes Generales are up, the new parliament then picks the PM, and Sánchez already flagged possible foreign interference — Chequeado stresses the housing controversy has created fertile ground for misleading narratives, online manipulation and disinformation around the crisis, government performance and electoral choice. GLOBAL PULSE: IBERIFIER on Brazil's runoff — AI-generated fake Veja magazine covers and fabricated GloboNews broadcasts claimed Lula had dedicated Brazil to Exu (an Afro-Brazilian deity) and that First Lady Janja planned to tax evangelical churches to fund Umbanda centres; no evidence either outlet ever published this, and Lupa identified the images as AI-generated (visual anomalies, recycled old edition numbers, inconsistencies). NORDIS on the US midterms — rumours that Texas Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico had been secretly admitted to a psychiatric facility after a week off the trail; Talarico attributed the absence to influenza and the Lindner Center of Hope in Ohio said he was never treated there, yet the claim spread on X via an unverified "hospital employee" audio recording and was personally amplified by Elon Musk, who has already spent $366m backing conservative candidates. IDMO on "cognitive security": a research strand arguing the real target of modern information operations is decision-making itself — uncertainty is amplified as information moves through institutional chains, turning tentative assessments into apparent certainties without new evidence; interest is growing at the EUISS and the GCSP, and countering manipulation requires understanding cognitive shortcuts, biases and interpretative frames rather than only content, with cases from Russian military provocations to AI use. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery (AIG-NCII / deepfake pornography) is a systemic threat to women in European politics, and the EU framework fails them three ways — it acts only after harm is done, it relies on victims finding the material and reporting it themselves, and its strongest duties bind only the largest platforms while most harm sits on small, often offshore sites — per 'Targeted and Ignored. Sexualised Deepfakes of Women MEPs and the Failure of Platform Responses' (Benjamin Shultz, Stefan Heumann, Torben Klausa, Vivien Benert).

- New issue: **Violence in France: The Far-Right Blames Ethnicity and Religion to Promote “Remigration”**
- Date: October 8, 2026
- URL: https://edmo.eu/publications/violence-in-france-the-far-right-blames-ethnicity-and-religion-to-promote-remigration/
- Total issues: 24
- Sections: ON THE RISE, ZOOM-IN, ELECTION BEAT, GLOBAL PULSE, ON A DIFFERENT NOTE (all 5)
- Note: Migration and the far right as the week's organising theme. ON THE RISE: the violence that swept France in late September–early October followed a familiar pattern — protests over teacher/staff shortages and deteriorating schools that broadened into a wider revolt over overcrowded classrooms, chaotic schedules, dilapidated buildings and educational inequality, turning most intense in the areas of greatest social hardship, where violent groups joined in and shifted the debate to vandalism and alleged repression; social media and the rise of the far right in Europe and the US added a new dimension, with a barrage of (sometimes old, unrelated) posts attributing the clashes not to those who burned vehicles and assaulted people but to an ethnicity or religion deemed inherently incompatible with European societies — “severe race riots,” per an AfD MP. Donald Trump weighed in: “This isn't about schools, this is about Islam wanting to take over a once great Country.” The editorial stresses that no reliable data support claims that Black, Arab or Muslim people are inherently more violent, that the over-representation in the imagery is better explained by hardship neighbourhoods also being home to second- and third-generation immigrant families, and that the far right's only proposed “solution” is remigration — disturbingly reminiscent of a century ago, when Nazi Germany first sought a Germany “free of Jews” and a few years later implemented the “Final Solution.” ZOOM-IN: NORDIS/TjekDet on viral TikTok videos claiming huge anti-immigration demonstrations in Madrid, Rome, Berlin, Lisbon, Paris, Dublin and London — most AI-generated, showing events that never happened or relabelling genuine protests, manufacturing a continent-wide mobilisation the evidence does not support; plus TjekDet debunking DF MEP Majbritt Birkholm's claim of 65 Swedish “no-go zones” (police recognise “vulnerable areas” but reject that citizens/services cannot enter). EDMO IRELAND/The Journal on Poland's exemption from the EU Migration Pact solidarity mechanism, invoked by Ireland's Aontú — Poland's is strictly conditional and one-year (roughly 953,000 Ukrainian refugees, 2020–2025 migratory pressure), Ireland's circumstances are not equivalent, and Ireland's indicative obligation is 648 relocations or €12.96m a year, though the precedent could be cited by others. NORDIS/TjekDet on Council of Europe Commissioner Michael O'Flaherty downplaying immigrant crime: researchers agree age, education, income and labour-market attachment substantially reduce the gap but warn against calling them the overwhelming explanation — 2024 non-Western male immigrants convicted 1.5x as often as men overall, non-Western male descendants 3x+ (about 2.5x after age adjustment, less with education/income but never eliminated), with caveats on ethnic profiling and police attention. GADMO debunking a German influencer's 22,787 deportations (2025) vs 1.48m “new immigrants” — the arrivals include ~192,000 German citizens and 457,000+ EU citizens, deportations are one departure category among many (voluntary departures, border refusals), and Afghans+Syrians were ~77,000, only 5.2%. ELECTION BEAT: BECID on Latvia's 2026 election — fear, not fraud claims, was the instrument: migrants/third-country nationals (~1.5% of the population) framed as criminals and threats despite lower crime rates, Kremlin-linked claims that support for Ukraine was provoking Russia, the Istanbul Convention used against LGBTQ/gender rights (false same-sex-marriage claim), and AI content reinforcing the narratives; IDMO on Latvian fugitive Ernests Rieksts (Fiji) promoting pro-Russian candidates via the fabricated-author-site Trieciens.com; FACT's TikTok data (88,445 posts, 514 accounts, 1.47m comments) finding no evidence of large-scale coordinated inauthentic behaviour but strong networks for populist/Russia-leaning parties (Latvija pirmajā vietā, Stabilitātei!, Suverēnā vara). FAKENEWS TRAGAČ via EDMO on Serbia's campaign (23 cases, identity/national themes targeting the student-led list, plus deceptive graphics that could spoil ballots ahead of the 25 Oct vote); MEDDMO on TikTok removing nine fake pro-Labour “news” accounts in Malta (433 followers, no link to the party established). GLOBAL PULSE: IBERIFIER/Chequeado on Brazil's 25 Oct Lula–Flávio Bolsonaro runoff after the 4 Oct first round, where AI deepfakes fabricated endorsements and voter testimonials despite new labelling rules; HDMO debunking a fake NABU “$140m cash raid” story on a cloned New Voice of Ukraine site; EDMO IRELAND/The Journal debunking an AI-altered “BBC News” video claiming the attempted Flydubai Dubai–Tel Aviv crash was an Indian-Israeli “false flag” to implicate Iran; DE FACTO on six “Chat Control” claims (the vote extended to 2028 the 2021 voluntary-scanning rule, separate from the unadopted CSAR; no mass surveillance, no effect on encrypted apps, “80% of arrests” misquotes investigations opened, “unanimous expert opposition” misleading, and it passed 314–276 because rejecting an extension needs an absolute majority); IBERIFIER on the Falklands/Malvinas debate revived by the Argentina–England World Cup semi-final. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: NewsGuard/IDMO — fake articles attributed to trustworthy outlets rose from 44 (H1 2025) to 421 (H1 2026), +850%, with 163 outlets imitated (BBC, Politico, Bloomberg News, Euronews, The Kyiv Independent, The Yorkshire Post, La Montagne).
