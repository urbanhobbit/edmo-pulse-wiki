## Trump mistakenly blames Ukraine alone for high diesel prices and adjusts U.S. foreign policy accordingly

**October 22, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

Trump mistakenly blames Ukraine alone for high diesel prices and adjusts U.S. foreign policy accordingly. ON THE RISE: With the US midterms approaching, the Trump administration blames soaring diesel prices on Ukrainian strikes against Russian oil refineries rather than on the war against Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, escalating its confrontation with Kyiv to unprecedented levels and even calling for Zelenskyy's removal. EDMO network fact-checks find the picture nuanced but not symmetric: Ukrainian attacks on refineries do affect global diesel prices, yet their impact is not comparable to that of the Iran war, and Kyiv's strikes are a response to bombardment by its aggressor. The deeper problem is the White House's willingness to announce a non-existent energy truce, cast Ukraine as the bad guy, and undermine Ukraine's air defences to force acceptance of its diktat. ZOOM-IN: BENEDMO/Factcheck.Vlanderen shows Russian growth in 2023-2025 was sustained by defence-industry investment and army recruitment, a stimulus now wearing off as sanctions bite; HDMO/Lakmusz tests Balázs Orbán's claim of record Hungarian fuel prices — MEKH data put the 1 October weekly average for 95-octane petrol at about 635 forints per litre, a level exceeded 19 times between 2022 and 2025; NORDIS/TjekDet reports Greenland rejecting Trump's Truth Social claim that extensive oil and gas exploration is about to begin, since no new licences have been granted since 2021. ELECTION BEAT: BROD finds Bulgaria's pre-election Facebook environment shaped by polarisation, distrust in institutions and emotionally charged manipulation narratives ahead of the 25 October presidential vote; CSD documents two pro-Kremlin Bulgarian-language Telegram networks circulating coordinated narratives within eight hours of drone strikes on two merchant ships in Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone on 6 October, tying the incident to the election debate via NewsFront and Vazrazhdane messaging; Istinomer/Amnesty International report systematic digital surveillance in Serbia — Pegasus and Novispy spyware, at least 14 people targeted since the start of 2026, including student movement members — as a tool of repression before the 25 October snap elections. GLOBAL PULSE: HDMO/Lakmusz reconstructs US State Department pressure that led the EU, Lithuania and Canada to withdraw support from the #Disinfo2026 conference in Vilnius over a panel on the US as a foreign information manipulation source, prompting an open letter by the European Partnership for Democracy and 80+ signatories, EDMO included, urging the European Parliament to defend editorial independence; GADMO/APA reports a significant rise in AI slop targeting Ukraine and Zelenskyy — videos mocking him with Trump, Putin and Kim Jong-un or portraying him as weak — which researchers say is becoming increasingly realistic; IBERIFIER covers Agencia Lupa and Aos Fatos fact-checking Brazil's first presidential runoff debate between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro ahead of the 25 October vote. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: the Center for Democracy & Technology (CDT) argues that the predictability of election misinformation lets Americans anticipate which false claims will appear at each stage of the 2026 midterms and build resilience.
