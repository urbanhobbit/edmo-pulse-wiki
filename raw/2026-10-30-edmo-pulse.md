## Brazil, Serbia, Israel: Election-related disinformation from Trump and Putin’s playbook

**October 30, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

Three major elections in one week — Brazil, Serbia and Israel — produced three different outcomes but one shared defining feature: disinformation aimed at delegitimising the democratic process and participation itself. In Brazil, Lula da Silva won the runoff after trailing Flávio Bolsonaro in the first round; Bolsonaro refused to concede despite a decisive margin, opened the floodgates to rigged-election conspiracy theories, was backed by Donald Trump, and then fled Brazil for the United States. In Serbia, President Aleksandar Vučić conceded immediately after 14 years in power — but explained the Students' List victory (52.7%) as foreign interference by several European countries and the American "deep state", using language that echoes Russian propaganda about colour revolutions. In Israel, Netanyahu dramatised election day on social media and claimed "Muslim Brotherhood supporters" were "storming polling stations and stealing the election"; Likud alleged Arab parties had "stolen" the vote, and police rejected the claim for lack of evidence. The editorial closes on the US midterms, where the administration is doing the same thing again, and warns that the rigged-election playbook "can easily end up in riots, uprisings and violence."

### ON THE RISE

**Weekly Watch of Emerging Disinformation Risks**

The week's organising theme is the globalisation of the "stolen election" narrative. EDMO frames the three contests as a natural experiment: Brazil's defeated candidate rejected a result beyond reasonable doubt; Serbia's defeated incumbent conceded but outsourced the explanation to foreign conspiracies; Israel's incumbent cast doubt on the count while voting was still under way.

- **Brazil.** Lula's runoff win — a comeback after trailing Flávio Bolsonaro in the first round — was met with a refusal to acknowledge defeat and mass circulation, in Brazil and internationally, of baseless claims that the election had been rigged and stolen. Bolsonaro received Donald Trump's support and subsequently left Brazil, finding refuge in the United States.
- **Serbia.** Vučić conceded, but attributed the Students' List victory (52.7%, a parliamentary majority) to interference by several European countries and the American "deep state" hostile to Trump. Prominent MAGA figures openly backed Vučić, who has long maintained friendly relations with Putin's Russia — Serbia being the only EU candidate country that has not sanctioned Moscow. Vučić called the result "the legitimisation of a revolution", a phrase EDMO reads as an echo of the Russian propaganda staple that the colour revolutions in Georgia, Armenia and Ukraine were Western-orchestrated coups.
- **Israel.** Netanyahu spent election day dramatising the vote on social media while polling stations remained open and circulated false content, claiming "Muslim Brotherhood supporters" were storming polling stations and stealing the election. After the vote, Likud alleged Arab parties had "stolen" the election and demanded an investigation; Israeli police rejected the claim, citing a lack of evidence.
- **United States.** Ahead of the 3 November midterms — with Trump lagging in the polls — officials sought to block certification of a Senate race in Alaska, threatened California election officials with imprisonment over their stance on mail-in ballots, and cast doubt on the legitimacy of Minnesota's Senate race should the Republican candidate lose. Trump promised a $1 million reward for reports of voter fraud in past and upcoming elections.

EDMO's conclusion is structural rather than episodic: whether the source is Russia (Serbia) or the United States (Israel, Brazil), "the lesson in how to delegitimize democracy when it fails to deliver the desired result" has been learned across the globe — and it is a dangerous game.

### ZOOM-IN

**A Closer Look at Cases Detected by the EDMO Network** — four contributions, all on the same underlying pattern: narratives that fuel polarisation and distrust in the vote.

**Serbia: The Student List was the most frequent target of disinformation** *(from EDMO)*
FakeNews Tragač analysed 59 disinformation cases between 9 September and 21 October 2026. The Students' List appears in two-thirds of cases, against 8% targeting the Serbian Progressive Party. Identity issues dominate — Kosovo and Metohija, Republika Srpska, sanctions against Russia, attitudes to Croatia and Albania — followed by narratives targeting the list's candidates and programme, with economic issues third. The dominant techniques are false attribution of statements or actions to candidates and groups, plus AI slop: ADMO highlights an AI-generated image of students displaying the Albanian double-headed eagle, meant to discredit the movement as nationally disloyal. Social media were the main channel, with outlets including Alo, Informer, B92, 24 Sedam and Večernje Novosti contributing significantly to spread.

**What do we know about the delay in closing the polls in the Brazilian elections?** *(from IBERIFIER)*
A viral claim — including on Telegram — held that Judge Cristiano Zanin, Lula's former lawyer, favoured Lula by extending voting until 19:00 in the 25 October runoff. Maldita.es shows the extension was not requested exclusively by Lula's campaign: Bolsonaro's representatives made similar requests because of heavy rain and access difficulties, and the Workers' Party cited public transport problems. Superior Electoral Tribunal data record roughly 34,330 votes after 17:00 — 0.03% of the total, against a margin of over four million votes. The fraud accusations are unfounded.

**Be careful with the results regarding the Israeli elections: there is still no definitive winner** *(from IBERIFIER)*
Claims of a landslide Netanyahu victory and an imminent mandate to form a government circulated while counting continued. Infoveritas, writing with 72% of votes scrutinised, puts Likud and its allies at 54 seats rather than the claimed 62 — against the 61 of 120 needed for a majority. No continuity claim was possible before the count finished; official results were expected on 4 November. Turnout reached 77.7%, and President Isaac Herzog came to the defence of Palestinian voters amid Likud's fraud allegations against parties representing them.

**Fact checks, fake stories and attempts to influence the US midterm elections** *(from NORDIS)*
TjekDet has begun compiling EFCSN- and IFCN-certified fact-checks on the 3 November 2026 midterms, in a continuously updated resource. Recent entries cover fake polls designed to disrupt the process, AI-manipulated videos featuring real people, AI-generated political ads, betting services fuelling fraud rumours, and false allegations against figures in both camps — including claims that Democrat Abdul El-Sayed is a jihadist and that a Republican senator blocked the release of Epstein-related documents.

### ELECTION BEAT

**Tracking electoral disinformation through EDMO Hubs**

**Spain: the AI campaign has already started with videos supporting the narrative of voter fraud in the November 29 mail-in vote** *(from EDMO)*
Pedro Sánchez's election announcement was followed by numerous deceptive AI-created videos on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and X: postal workers shredding ballots, votes being replaced with PSOE ballots, election officials manipulating mail-in votes. Some are labelled as AI-generated, satire or digital content, yet they reinforce long-standing vote-rigging narratives centred on postal voting. Maldita.es warns the content is being amplified through social media and private messaging and risks severely undermining trust in the Spanish democratic process.

**An old video does not prove a plot to get "Muslims" in Belgium to vote in Spain's election** *(from IBERIFIER)*
EFE Verifica debunks claims that Spain's embassy or consulate in Belgium is enabling foreign "Muslims" with no connection to Spain to vote on 29 November 2026. The claim rests on an old video filmed outside the Spanish Consulate in Brussels that already circulated — and was already debunked — during the 2024 European Parliament elections. The footage shows no evidence of fraud or of the individuals voting; Spanish law does not allow citizenship to be granted arbitrarily, requiring strict residency and naturalisation conditions. Overseas voting for the November election had not yet opened when the claim began circulating.

### GLOBAL PULSE

**Disinformation narratives shaping the world's conversations**

**What does the Palestinian flag have to do with youth protests in Europe?** *(from IDMO)*
For many young protesters the Palestinian flag has evolved from a symbol of solidarity with Gaza into a broader emblem of opposition to inequality, precarity, repression and perceived social injustice — a transnational protest symbol, per journalist Leonardo Bianchi and podcaster Leila Belhadj Mohamed, resonating with a generation facing shared economic and political grievances. Facta shows how right-wing and pro-Israel commentators use the flag's presence at demonstrations in France and Italy to portray unrest as Islamist, anti-Western or migrant-driven, oversimplifying a more complex social movement and turning the flag into a focal point of culture-war narratives on identity, migration and public protest.

**Could social media platforms be suspended during public disorder?** *(from DE FACTO)*
The fact-check examines former French PM Gabriel Attal's proposal to temporarily block platforms in areas affected by riots after violence linked to the 17 October protests. Such measures are not legally impossible but face significant obstacles under French and EU law: the Conseil d'État annulled the 2024 TikTok suspension in New Caledonia because it was not sufficiently time-limited and alternatives had not been exhausted. Under the DSA, blocking TikTok, Instagram or X is a measure of last resort requiring proof that less restrictive moderation and enforcement failed. No EU member state has adopted such measures in comparable situations, given the high legal threshold and fundamental-rights concerns.

**Political campaigning moves into Facebook's blind spots** *(from BROD)*
Funky Citizens finds that Meta's EU political advertising ban has not eliminated political ads on Facebook but made them less transparent. Of 568 paid advertisements from 26 Romanian Facebook pages in 2025–2026, 437 contained political content — electoral messaging, communist nostalgia — while only 21 were labelled political. Researchers identified networks of seemingly coordinated pages with non-political names, many sharing administrators based in Ireland, publishing near-identical content and launching campaigns simultaneously. Meta removed or blocked 90 ads, but some were quickly reposted by other pages and evaded detection. Because most ads are not classified as political, spending data remain unavailable, making independent assessment of scale and funding impossible.

### ON A DIFFERENT NOTE

Accusations of violated fair electoral procedures rank among the most significant topics of recent campaigns: electoral integrity was the most common subject of debunked false information in a study of the 2024 European Parliament elections — over 20% of instances overall, and over 80% in Germany. The reading recommendation is Ofcom's **Understanding online information-based threats to elections**.
