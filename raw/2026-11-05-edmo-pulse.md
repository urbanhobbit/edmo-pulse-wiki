## Poland's constitutional crisis: between foreign interference and genuine alignments

**November 5, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

Poland's deepening constitutional crisis is the week's case study in how complex domestic dynamics get oversimplified by national and international actors fostering polarisation, how extremism and disinformation thrive in that environment, and how foreign actors exploit it for their own ends. EDMO's central caution, drawn from Demagog.pl, is methodological: "not everything happens because of Russian interference and disinformation" — and politicians whose positions align with Russia's are not necessarily Russian agents. At the same time, the de facto alignment of the European far right, Russia and parts of the US right on the same narrative ("a judicial coup by globalist, liberal forces backed by the EU") is, in EDMO's words, now undeniable.

### ON THE RISE

**Weekly Watch of Emerging Disinformation Risks**

*How did we get here?* The roots go back to 2015: outgoing Civic Platform (PO) tried to appoint five Constitutional Tribunal judges instead of the three it was entitled to; President Andrzej Duda refused to recognise any, and the incoming PiS majority appointed five alternatives, including replacements for three lawfully filled seats. The Tribunal ruled three PO appointments valid and two premature; PiS proceeded regardless and legislated for the early re-election of the Tribunal's President. PO contributed to the initial dispute, but EDMO assigns principal responsibility for the systemic crisis to PiS.

- **2021–2025, external rulings.** The ECtHR held in *Xero Flor v. Poland* that a panel including an improperly appointed judge was not a tribunal established by law; in December 2025 the CJEU found Poland in breach of EU law over the Tribunal's composition and its rejection of fundamental EU principles (*Case C-448/23*).
- **2023–2026, unfinished repair.** Donald Tusk's return did not resolve the crisis: after initially refusing to recognise the Tribunal or publish its rulings, the Civic Coalition shifted course as judicial terms expired. On 13 March 2026 the Sejm elected six judges, but President Karol Nawrocki, backed by PiS, accepted only two oaths, leaving the Tribunal with eleven members. The remaining four took their oaths in the Sejm on 9 April and formally transmitted them to the Presidential Chancellery — Nawrocki and Tribunal President Bogdan Święczkowski continued to exclude them. In May the ECtHR issued interim measures requiring authorities not to obstruct the judges (*Dziurda and Others v. Poland*).
- **13 October 2026, Venice Commission.** Opinion No. 1294/2026 clarified that the President has no discretion to review parliamentary appointments and that oaths may be taken without his physical participation if he refuses or delays. Nawrocki maintained his refusal.
- **30 October 2026, escalation.** Senior judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska suspended Święczkowski and his deputy for thirty days, assumed leadership of the Tribunal and barred Święczkowski from the building. The legal basis for her intervention remains disputed. Tusk and Nawrocki exchanged accusations; Nawrocki called the suspension an unconstitutional seizure of a state institution and convened consultations with parliamentary parties, which produced no accepted compromise as of 2 November.

*What are the different actors saying?* The confrontation acquired an international dimension fast. Viktor Orbán, Geert Wilders, Marine Le Pen, Jordan Bardella, George Simion and Nigel Farage circulated statements defending Nawrocki's position, some denouncing a "coup" by Tusk and the EU. MAGA-associated influencers and personalities portrayed the events as a judicial coup by Tusk and the European Union against conservative forces, and spread disinformation. Russia positioned itself too: on 31 October Dmitry Medvedev, Deputy Chairman of Russia's Security Council, called the events a "constitutional coup" and accused "the liberals around Tusk, Brussels' protégé" of oppressing PiS conservatives. Tusk replied that "now it's all clear", framed the confrontation geopolitically as advancing Russian strategic interests, and alleged that pro-Russian forces and Russian social-media bots were backing one side — though, as EDMO notes, no publicly verifiable evidence has yet established direct Russian involvement in Nawrocki's actions. Demagog.pl warns that mutual accusations of being agents of influence, when unsupported by credible evidence, deepen polarisation and erode trust in public debate.

*Far-right, United States and Russia, again.* EDMO reads the Polish case as the third iteration of a recurring scheme: the Ceuta crisis in August was not prompted by any of these actors, yet all of them jumped on it with the same narratives (the EU is doomed; Muslim migrants are violent parasites destroying white-Christian societies; the Great Replacement is happening); the September youth protests in France and elsewhere were not caused by the US, the European far right or Russian-linked networks, yet they were immediately framed as a violent uprising of Muslim migrants to be "remigrated". Poland now repeats the pattern with MAGA influencers rather than the administration. The alignment is both ideological and practical: Russia wants to weaken EU states that support Ukraine and bolster far-right parties that would end that support; far-right forces want to persuade voters their Eurosceptic, anti-Ukrainian, pro-Russian stance is reasonable; the US wants to weaken the EU as a "transnational body that undermines political liberty and sovereignty" and support ideologically aligned forces. European societies are therefore targeted by overlapping and mutually reinforcing messages.

### ZOOM-IN

**A Closer Look at Cases Detected by the EDMO Network** — three complementary perspectives on the Polish crisis.

**The rule of law dispute: how to end the conflict over "Neo-Judges"?** *(from CEDMO)*
Demagog.pl's October 2024 analysis of the status of "neo-judges" appointed after the 2017 reform of the National Council of the Judiciary (KRS) weighed judicial independence, constitutional legitimacy and legal certainty. Justice Minister Adam Bodnar proposed a differentiated review of appointments; the Venice Commission stressed individual assessments, proportionality and the right of appeal. Experts saw political compromise as unlikely and listed four scenarios — negotiated reform, prolonged stalemate, pragmatic political resolution or "cold war" — judging the last most probable and warning it "could result in a Polish equivalent of the storming of the Capitol on January 6, 2021". EDMO notes the "bewildering echo" of that prediction in today's competing institutional loyalties.

**A soldier declares his readiness to defend the tribunal? This could be a fake account** *(from CEDMO)*
An X profile presenting itself as "Powel", a former non-commissioned officer in the Polish army, drew hundreds of thousands of views by declaring readiness to defend Poland and its constitutional order in support of President Nawrocki. Demagog.pl finds several red flags: the account was created in October 2026 and has already published a large volume of controversial content, including anti-EU and antisemitic material; it is managed from Georgia; and the profile image appears AI-generated, as suggested by a SynthID watermark. Demagog also notes that some allegations levelled against the profile by Anonymus are themselves inaccurate — a reminder that verification matters most when institutional tensions can escalate on the back of inauthentic statements.

**Poland's rule-of-law crisis revisited: The story of juristic scientism** *(from EDMO/EUI)*
Wojciech Zomerski (Visiting Fellow, EUI; Associated Researcher, University of Wrocław) argues the roots of the crisis go deeper than the 2015 populist turn, growing from enduring deficiencies in Poland's legal culture in an integrative, agentic-structural reading. The key concept is "juristic scientism" — a legal imaginary assuming professionals can uncover the "true" and "objective" meaning of law through rigorous scientific method. Constructed under communism as a defence of legal autonomy against authoritarianism, it persisted into democracy and hindered a robust rule-of-law culture, producing excessive formalism, poor-quality statements of reasons and an inability to convince the public — ultimately undermining the sense of justice itself.

### ELECTION BEAT

**Tracking electoral disinformation through EDMO Hubs** — Spain and Bulgaria, both heading into votes.

**Preliminary report ahead of 2026 general elections in Spain** *(from IBERIFIER)*
IBERIFIER's pre-election report identifies threats to the integrity of the electoral debate: generative AI content, impersonation of established media outlets and use of private messaging platforms. Researchers warn that Spanish-language disinformation travels fast, with narratives targeting democratic institutions and migration, and note rises in AI-generated deepfakes, manipulated videos and cloned voices — in a media environment where only 33% of Spaniards trust the news and 74% struggle to distinguish true from false information online. The report recalls patterns from earlier cycles: claims of voting manipulation in the 2023 general elections and Russian-linked operations targeting the 2024 European elections, warning that altered political content blurs the line between real and manipulated material and erodes trust in truthful evidence.

**IBERIFIER activates Rapid Response System against electoral disinformation** *(from IBERIFIER)*
IBERIFIER has activated the EU Rapid Response System (RRS) ahead of the 29 November elections — the mechanism's first activation in Spain, since the DSA entered into force just after the previous general election. Monitoring runs from 2 November to 6 December, focused on viral disinformation campaigns, information manipulation and foreign interference. Notifications to platforms follow neutrality, independence and rigour, and are submitted only after verification against reliable sources; the mechanism neither limits freedom of expression, nor allows the observatory to remove content, and neither national governments nor the European Commission are involved in identifying content.

**Bulgaria's pre-presidential election Facebook information environment** *(from BROD)*
The second issue of BROD's Narrative Atlas examines Bulgaria's Facebook environment just before the 25 October presidential election. Discourse moved towards campaign-financing controversies, candidates' participation in debates and Black Sea maritime security incidents; most narratives centred on acting president Iliana Iotova's refusal to debate and on claims that certain candidate lists were registered to claim public subsidies. Post-euro accession concerns and the cost of living were used to mobilise discontent. BROD also finds a visibility disparity: confrontational commentary and investigative publications out-perform official communications from electoral authorities. Its conclusion: a polarised environment, amplified emotionally charged content and challenged institutional trust — with a plea to distinguish verified claims from misleading information and political satire.

### GLOBAL PULSE

**Disinformation narratives shaping the world's conversations**

**Egypt launches AI fact-checking chatbot based on government-approved information** *(from IDMO)*
Facta reports on "Chat Egypt", an AI-powered fact-checking chatbot presented by the Egyptian government as a tool to counter online misinformation using official state sources. Facta argues it is built around government-approved narratives rather than independent verification, and has already shown shortcomings: it failed to detect a manipulated image and omitted or refused sensitive issues such as Egypt's poor press-freedom ranking. The launch comes amid an escalating crackdown on independent journalism under President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi — six fact-checkers arrested, allegations of torture, journalists prosecuted, independent outlets closed — so critics read Chat Egypt as part of an effort to control information rather than a genuine fact-checking initiative.

**Artificial intelligence created new problems in the US election** *(from NORDIS)*
The 2026 US midterms showed both the influence and the risk of AI chatbots as political information sources. Researchers found major chatbots sometimes gave inaccurate or misleading answers — factual errors, fabricated quotations, incorrect voting guidance, repetition of false narratives tied to foreign influence campaigns. Studies also showed chatbots can play a constructive role by rejecting common election conspiracy theories and directing users to verified information. The lesson: treat AI-generated political information critically and verify important claims against trusted sources.

**No evidence for Trump's claims of mass non-citizen voting** *(from BENEDMO)*
Factcheck.Vlaanderen examines Donald Trump's repeated claims that large numbers of non-citizens vote illegally in US elections. While isolated cases exist, there is no evidence the practice is widespread or capable of affecting outcomes; multiple studies, audits and investigations show such cases are extremely rare, largely because registration systems contain safeguards and penalties for illegal voting are severe. The allegations are unsupported by available evidence and feed a misleading narrative about election integrity.
