## How disinformation exploited the Kraków tragedy to attack the usual targets, and how Elon Musk made things worse

**November 19, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

This edition's lead case is the mass shooting in Kraków on 7 November 2026, used by EDMO as a specimen of the recurring "mass-shooting information disorder" pattern it first mapped in the 30 April Weekly Pulse: within hours of a lethal attack, competing online factions fill the vacuum of facts with identity claims about the perpetrator, each claim tailored to a political target. EDMO's diagnosis is not that the public is misinformed but that the attribution vacuum itself is weaponised — Polish and foreign actors (the latter notably Russia) use the shooter's alleged nationality to attack Ukrainian refugees, while actors on both sides of the Atlantic use his alleged ideology to attack the left or the far right. The distinctive element of this edition is the platform layer: X is the only major platform that, according to Polish authorities, did not respond to a single report on illegal or disinformation content connected to the shooting, and Elon Musk personally amplified several demonstrably false items.

### ON THE RISE

**Weekly Watch of Emerging Disinformation Risks**

On 7 November 2026 a 24-year-old Polish man, **Michał Oksiński**, drove into Kraków's historic Main Market Square and opened fire on the crowded area. Six people were killed and 15 injured, including two police officers. The attacker was wounded in an exchange of gunfire with police and died in hospital on 9 November. The same day, the bodies of his parents and his 21-year-old sister were found at the family home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski; investigators believe he was responsible.

These are the facts. Who Oksiński was and why he acted became, within hours, objects of speculation and disinformation. EDMO's Polish hub **Demagog.pl** debunked a series of demonstrably false claims about his identity, ideology, political views and affiliations:

- that he was not Polish but **Ukrainian**, or had at least fought in Ukraine and wore Ukrainian patches during the attack;
- that he was an **LGBTQ+ activist** who had taken part in Pride demonstrations;
- that he was a **leftist** who had marched in anti-racist protests.

Immediately after the attack, Interior Minister **Marcin Kierwiński** suggested Oksiński might have been fascinated by far-right ideologies. Later evidence pointed in that direction — online searches relating to Anders Behring Breivik and the presence of far-right symbols — but the investigation is ongoing, and EDMO notes that ministerial statements about ideology without solid evidence only added noise to an already chaotic information environment.

**The recurring pattern.** Whenever a lethal mass shooting occurs, online factions exploit the event to attack their targets and political adversaries, and false content circulates quickly to support the resulting theories. In Kraków, the "Ukrainian" claim served two constituencies at once: domestic actors exploiting tensions between Poles and the large Ukrainian refugee community, and foreign actors — Russia in particular — that consistently spread anti-Ukraine disinformation to erode European support for Kyiv. The "LGBTQ+/left-wing" claim served far-right forces on both sides of the Atlantic.

**The role of X and Elon Musk.** EDMO is unusually direct here. Community Notes repeatedly fail to counter the systemic circulation of false information on the platform, and the Kraków claims were no exception: the vast majority circulated widely and for a long time without any note. According to Polish authorities, X is the only major platform that — unlike Meta or TikTok — "has not responded to a single report regarding illegal and disinformation content" related to the shooting. Musk, whose account is followed by 240 million users, actively amplified false content supporting the conspiracy theory that Oksiński was a leftist: he shared the claim that Oksiński had joined anti-racist marches, and amplified a conspiracy account's post asserting that the BBC had called the incident a "far-right shooting" while the attacker was allegedly a far-left radical and communist sympathiser — accusing the broadcaster of spreading propaganda. He also shared the claim that the attacker was a left-wing extremist who supported abortion rights and the LGBTQ+ community while harbouring hostility towards Christianity.

### ZOOM-IN

**A Closer Look at Cases Detected by the EDMO Network** — three hubs, three fabricated "evidences" about the same perpetrator.

EDMO recalls its 30 April Weekly Pulse, which examined how mass shootings trigger harmful information trends: political manipulation, identity-based scapegoating, discrimination against vulnerable groups and the global spread of "crime" digital sub-cultures. The Kraków case confirms the pattern, with far-right actors, pro-Kremlin sources and social-media commentators spreading competing narratives across borders about the perpetrator's political or Ukrainian connections.

**Unfounded rumours spread about the motive behind the mass murder in Poland** *(from NORDIS)*
Polish officials publicly commented on Oksiński's alleged links to right-wing extremism, citing his interest in Anders Breivik — the Norwegian who murdered 77 people in 2011 and became an inspiration in far-right circles, including for the 2019 Christchurch attacker. Claims of left-wing extremism nevertheless spread widely. According to **Källkritikbyrån**'s analysis, they rested in particular on a photograph taken at an anti-racist demonstration in Łódź in March 2026, allegedly showing Oksiński among the participants. Independent fact-checks established that the person in the photo is someone else, and traced the circulation of the claim and the image to **Herman Tertsch**, a former Spanish journalist based in Warsaw and now an MEP for the Spanish far-right party Vox. In a parallel GADMO investigation, **AFP** confirms that the young man holding a "Refugees Welcome" banner at the March 2026 Łódź demonstration is not Michał Oksiński but another person, identified as Maksymilian, who told AFP he intends to take legal action over the disinformation spread about him. The article stresses that the investigation is ongoing and that conclusions are premature.

**AI-modified images spread as 'evidence' of Polish shooter's political beliefs** *(from EDMO Ireland)*
*The Journal* examines how Irish far-right actors contributed to the unfounded claims, focusing on **Derek Blighe**, founder of the far-right party Ireland First and a former election candidate. On 8 November Blighe published a Facebook post claiming Oksiński was a radical left-wing, antifascist and anarcho-communist activist. Accompanied by a collage of four images, the post suggested the attack had been wrongly attributed to the far right; it was later shared thousands of times. The investigation found that some images were over five years old and others had been manipulated with artificial intelligence, including images carrying **SynthID** watermarks. The claims originated from a Polish-language X account posting similar allegations. The images provide no reliable evidence of left-wing sympathies or political motives.

**Georg Spöttle also found the Ukrainian thread in the Kraków attack, sharing a suspicious AI image from a strange Telegram channel** *(from HDMO)*
**Lakmusz** analyses fabricated evidence supporting the claim that Oksiński had links to Ukraine. Hungarian commentator **Georg Spöttle** shared a Facebook image of an armed, masked man wearing a vest bearing the Ukrainian flag, suggesting a connection with the perpetrator. Fact-checks showed the image was AI-generated or AI-manipulated. The Telegram channel where it first appeared could not be reliably linked to Oksiński; on the contrary, its content appears to have been repeatedly modified, including after Oksiński's arrest and death. The article reconstructs how earlier insinuations about Ukrainian connections spread, fuelled by sources close to Russian propaganda; according to a Polish investigation, the narratives could be traced to a pro-Russian disinformation operation aimed at implicating Ukraine in the tragedy.

### ELECTION BEAT

**Tracking electoral disinformation through EDMO Hubs** — Spain, ten days out from the 29 November vote, and Russia is back with a branded operation.

**The Russian operation "Matryoshka" launches a campaign to interfere in Spain's upcoming election** *(from IBERIFIER)*
*Maldita.es* documents a new campaign by the Russian operation **Matryoshka**, aimed at influencing public debate through coordinated false content and **media impersonation**. The campaign fabricates articles and videos mimicking established Spanish outlets — El País, El Mundo — as well as international ones: AFP, Reuters, Euronews, France 24 and The Economist. The content is then amplified through bot accounts on **X and Bluesky**. According to **Antibot4Navalny**, the goal is to present Spain's elections as ineffective, to tie Europe's economic problems to support for Ukraine and to sanctions against Russia, and to advocate fragmentation including Catalan independence. Antibot4Navalny identifies three narrative blocks:

- **"Useless elections"** — "the housing crisis is just the tip of the iceberg: the economy is doomed, the elections will not help";
- **"Redirecting funds meant for Ukraine and abandoning the EU"** — "Europe's economic collapse is a direct result of financial support for Ukraine";
- **Anti-Russian sanctions and "fragmentation of Europe to block Brussels"** — that Germany should again be split into East and West, that Catalonia should demand independence.

Matryoshka (a.k.a. Operation Overload when it adds mass mailings to fact-checkers and newsrooms) was exposed in January 2024 by AFP Factuel and Antibot4Navalny, has circulated on X since at least September 2023, and targets the reply sections of media, public figures and fact-checking organisations in more than 60 countries. It used the same playbook after the 28 April 2025 Iberian blackout (impersonating The Independent and France 24) and for this campaign is already known to have produced at least one earlier, single-topic Maldita piece on 13 November.

**Spain general election 2026: IBERIFIER's weekly report 1** *(from IBERIFIER)*
The first election-monitoring report, covering 2–6 November, identified misleading claims that emerged after the resignation of Transport Minister **Óscar Puente** following a fatal train crash in Urda. False messages on X stated that electoral lists could not be modified after registration, or that voters would have to cross out Puente's name on the ballot for their vote to count — misleading interpretations of the electoral rules. The report also documents AI-generated interference: a widely shared image mimicking an El País front page with polling data showing a huge rise for PSOE, and another fabricated image of an allegedly empty PSOE campaign rally.

**Spain general election 2026: IBERIFIER's weekly report 2** *(from IBERIFIER)*
The second report covers 7–13 November and flags three possible signals to electoral integrity, all still under review. Its focus is procedural: misleading claims about ballot validity and messages impersonating official voting notifications; decontextualised and recycled content, including outdated information on public funding and party affiliations; and misleading figures on immigration, gender equality, LGBTI rights and the alleged foreign links of parties. That week also saw the launch of **Comprobado 29N**, an alliance of Spanish fact-checking organisations coordinating verification until 6 December.

### GLOBAL PULSE

**Disinformation narratives shaping the world's conversations** — a climate-theory-of-everything, a Black Sea narrative battle and a leaked propagandist's file.

**The atmosphere is not "saturated"; excess CO₂ continues to cause warming** *(from BENEDMO)*
A fact-check by **Factcheck.Vlaanderen** rejects the claim that the atmosphere can become "saturated" with CO₂ so that additional emissions no longer cause warming — a claim promoted by the US think tank **The Heartland Institute**, which has a history of challenging the scientific consensus, opposing environmental policy and receiving funding linked to fossil-fuel donors including ExxonMobil. Climate scientists cited in the fact-check explain that rising CO₂ concentrations continue to strengthen the greenhouse effect: although CO₂ already absorbs much infrared radiation at certain wavelengths, additional CO₂ still makes it harder for heat to escape into space. The "CO₂ saturation" theory is not supported by current climate science.

**Narratives from the Black Sea: drone attacks trigger battle over narratives in Romania and Bulgaria** *(from BROD)*
Between 5 and 23 October 2026, drone attacks on commercial vessels in the Black Sea triggered competing narratives in Romania and Bulgaria. A report by BROD partner **Funky Citizens** examines how the information environment evolved around the incidents — who was to blame, whether the ships were legitimate targets, NATO's capacity to provide security, and the risk of escalation. Pro-Kremlin narratives adapted rapidly to the attacks, promoting unsupported claims before official information was available; delays in coordinated institutional responses created the window in which those narratives gained traction and shaped perceptions of the unfolding security crisis.

**Leaked documents reveal how pro-Kremlin propagandist J.M. Dougan was used and discarded by Russian intelligence** *(from IDMO)*
Leaked documents published by **The Insider** offer new insight into **John Mark Dougan**, a former Florida deputy sheriff who fled to Moscow in 2016. According to the investigation, Dougan had grown frustrated with his role in Russian influence operations, complaining about workload, low pay and the constraints of working in Russia. The files document the mechanics of his activity: false allegations against former US vice-presidential candidate **Tim Walz**, a network of **167 fake local news websites**, and efforts to spread false narratives about Ukraine. The findings largely reinforce earlier reporting on his role in pro-Kremlin campaigns.

### ON A DIFFERENT NOTE

*The digitalisation of society is fundamentally changing how people approach entertainment, interpersonal communication and information consumption. Shifting traditional activities online removes barriers of time and space, but at the same time significantly lowers the threshold for risky behaviour and deepens the population's psychological and financial vulnerability.*
