## Fake doctors, real scams: health disinformation exploits desperation

**September 17, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

Health disinformation as an industry of false hope. EDMO monitoring puts health-related disinformation at 7% of all detected EU disinformation in both July and August, with Covid-19 monitored separately at 2% (July) and 4% (August). ON THE RISE argues that 'disinformation about miraculous cures' is the most despicable subcategory - preying on the desperation of people facing serious illness - and that it now operates as an industrial-scale business: AI-generated fake doctors and experts, fabricated studies, false news, videos and images, monetised through platform schemes and amplified by disinformation influencers for whom the lies simply go viral. It places this among other health narratives (sunscreen-causes-cancer claims, Hantavirus fearmongering, the evergreen 'mammography causes cancer' hoax, Trump-boosted vaccine-autism claims) and blames the consistent inability to protect people from scams and demonstrably false content, while noting that regulating US-based platforms is now politically and economically harder than ever for the EU - though it is the right thing to do and can literally save lives. ZOOM-IN: NORDIS/TjekDet expose a network of more than 1,000 AI-generated 'doctors' on YouTube producing around 100,000 misleading videos in 30 languages (fictional personas such as Sofie Larsen advising older men on prostate problems and sexual health, channels monetised via subscriptions, donations and supplement or book sales, some abusing real hospital brands) and list the warning signs of bogus health experts: no uncertainty, emotional language, personal attacks on critics, cherry-picked evidence. BENEDMO debunks TikTok claims that magnesium treats ADHD - lower average magnesium levels in children with ADHD are an association, not causation, studies are inconsistent and evidence for supplements is weak. CEDMO fact-checks viral claims that the anti-parasitic drug mebendazole is a hidden cancer cure: the 2021 Johns Hopkins patent does not prove efficacy, human trials are lacking, and self-medication risks drug interactions and requires liver and anaemia monitoring. ELECTION BEAT: NORDIS/Kallkritikbyran - political scientists Jan Teorell and John Hogstrom say nothing indicates deliberate voter fraud in Sweden despite viral missing-ballot videos (replenishment delays, voters removing rival ballots; a 2022 study found some 40% of election officials had witnessed ballot-paper sabotage), plus the recurring false claim that Election Authority head Anna Nyqvist is a Social Democrat (she has no role in vote counting); Sweden Democrats in Atvidaberg criticised a polling site for turning away a voter over political clothing. BECID - ahead of Latvia's October 3 parliamentary election, four parties fight over the fractured Russian-speaking vote once unified under the collapsed 'Harmony': 'Harmony Center' rebrands around ex-mayor Usakovs's legacy and accuses splinter rivals of betrayal; 'For Stability!' (2022 winner) now frames Ukraine aid as unwanted foreign entanglement; Stepanenko's 'Sovereign Power', polling around 11.8%, pledges to redirect defence spending and end conscription; the ethnically Latvian LPV under Slesers courts Russian speakers more cautiously over new residency language rules. GADMO - German claims that AfD's drop from 51.2% in ballot-box votes to 24.3% by post in Saxony-Anhalt proves postal-vote manipulation; the figures are accurate but no fraud is evidenced (the same pattern appeared in the 2025 Bundestag election, 25.3% vs 13.2%), researcher Simon Franzmann attributes the gap to voter demographics and AfD's own long-standing advice to vote in person, and a staged video falsely depicted destroyed ballots. GLOBAL PULSE: EDMO IRELAND/FactCheck reviews four claims made by Donald Trump in Ireland - that Ireland 'took' US pharmaceutical companies and is 'fleecing' America, that Irish investment in the US nearly doubled, that the Ukraine war is the primary cause of global fuel shortages, and that the US could afford $5,000 per adult - finding facts mixed with misleading context or largely unsupported. IDMO/Facta - the assassination of Charlie Kirk has spawned conspiracy theories inside MAGA itself, with Candace Owens amplifying allegations about Erika Kirk's succession role, turning Kirk's legacy into an internal information war of false claims, harassment and extremist rhetoric that could reshape the movement. IDMO/Facta - Iran floods social platforms with AI-generated videos (fictional English-speaking female soldiers from near-identical accounts created around the same time) to project military strength and legitimacy, some unlabelled, blurring the line between reality and propaganda. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: MIT research 'Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task' warns of cognitive debt and the need to consider effects on critical thinking and intellectual independence.
