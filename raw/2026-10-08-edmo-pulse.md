## Violence in France: The Far-Right Blames Ethnicity and Religion to Promote “Remigration”

**October 8, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

Violence in France: The Far-Right Blames Ethnicity and Religion to Promote “Remigration”. ON THE RISE: The violence that swept France between late September and early October followed a now familiar European pattern - protests that began with concrete grievances (teacher and staff shortages, deteriorating conditions in many schools) and quickly widened into a broader revolt over overcrowded classrooms, chaotic schedules, dilapidated buildings, educational inequality and uncertainty about the future, becoming most intense in the areas of greatest social hardship, where violent groups joined in and pulled the debate towards criminal vandalism and allegations of violent repression. Social media and the rise of the far right in Europe and the United States added another dimension: a deafening barrage of posts - sometimes old and unrelated - blamed the clashes not on the individuals who burned vehicles and assaulted people but on an ethnicity or religion presented as inherently incompatible with European societies ('severe race riots', in the words of an AfD MP). The fact that many of those shown in videos and images are people of colour and/or Muslim is explained not by the fact that the hardest-hit neighbourhoods with the most run-down schools are also home to second- and third-generation immigrant families, but by an alleged intrinsic difference of migrant descendants and Muslims; Donald Trump joined in, posting that 'This isn't about schools, this is about Islam wanting to take over a once great Country'. France and its political class are blamed, possible failures of the integration model and budgetary choices are ignored, and no reliable data are offered for the claim that Black, Arab or Muslim people are inherently more violent; the only 'solution' proposed is the far right's remigration - a call disturbingly reminiscent of what was said a century ago about other ethnicities and religions, when Nazi Germany first sought a Germany 'free of Jews' and only a few years later implemented its 'Final Solution'. ZOOM-IN: migration-related claims frequently combine genuine social and political concerns with misleading representations of protest, crime, immigration policy and asylum responsibilities. NORDIS/TjekDet investigated viral TikTok videos claiming to show huge anti-immigration demonstrations across Europe (Madrid, Rome, Berlin, Lisbon, Paris, Dublin, London): most are AI-generated, showing events that never occurred or relabelling genuine protests as anti-immigration rallies, creating the impression of a continent-wide mobilisation the evidence does not support; a second TjekDet piece dismantles the claim by Dansk Folkeparti MEP Majbritt Birkholm that Sweden has 65 'no-go zones' where ordinary Swedes and emergency services cannot enter (Swedish police recognise 'vulnerable areas' with concentrated criminality but reject the idea that citizens or emergency services cannot enter). EDMO IRELAND/The Journal analyses Poland's exemption from the EU Migration Pact solidarity mechanism, invoked by Ireland's Aontú to argue Ireland should be exempt too: Poland's exemption is strictly conditional and limited to one year, granted after the Commission judged it faced significant 2020-2025 migratory pressure, largely from roughly 953,000 Ukrainian refugees, while Ireland's circumstances are not equivalent (most foreign-born residents there are neither asylum seekers nor refugees) - though the Polish precedent could be invoked by other states; Ireland's indicative obligation is currently 648 relocations or 12.96 million euros a year. NORDIS/TjekDet examines Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights Michael O'Flaherty's claim that immigrants' and descendants' over-representation in Danish crime statistics is largely explained by demographic and socioeconomic factors: researchers agree age, education, income and labour-market attachment significantly reduce the disparity but caution against calling them the overwhelming explanation - in 2024 non-Western male immigrants were convicted 1.5 times as often as the male population overall and non-Western male descendants more than three times as often (about 2.5 times after adjusting for age, lower again with education and income, but the disparity is never eliminated), with caveats about possible ethnic profiling and differences in police attention. GADMO debunks a German far-right influencer's comparison of 22,787 deportations in 2025 with 1.48 million 'new immigrants' (mainly Afghans and Syrians) as measuring fundamentally different things: the arrivals include nearly 192,000 German citizens and more than 457,000 EU citizens, deportations are only one category of departures (tens of thousands also left voluntarily or were refused entry at the borders), and Afghans and Syrians together accounted for about 77,000 arrivals, just 5.2% of the total. ELECTION BEAT: BECID's report on Latvia's 2026 parliamentary election finds fear, rather than election-fraud claims, was the central disinformation instrument - migrants and third-country nationals (around 1.5% of the population) portrayed as criminals and threats to women, identity and security despite committing crimes at lower rates than Latvian citizens; Kremlin-associated narratives that Latvia's support for Ukraine and military build-up was provoking Russia; the Istanbul Convention used to stoke LGBTQ and gender fears, including the false claim it would require Latvia to legalise same-sex marriage; and AI-generated imagery and fabricated videos reinforcing the emotional narratives. A parallel IDMO investigation covers Ernests Rieksts, a Latvian fugitive living in Fiji who promoted pro-Russian candidates through the fabricated-author-profile website Trieciens.com. FACT analyses TikTok activity around Latvia's election - 88,445 posts from 514 accounts and 1.47 million comments - finding no immediate evidence of large-scale automated reposting or coordinated inauthentic behaviour, but major differences in party organisation and reach, with populist and Russia-leaning parties (Latvija pirmaja vieta, Stabilatatei!, Suverena vara) having the strongest networks; reusable scripts, hashtags, candidate networks and highly active commenters appear, though engagement patterns alone do not prove manipulation. FAKENEWS TRAGAC via EDMO reports that three weeks into Serbia's 2026 campaign the 23 detected election-related disinformation cases centred on identity and national issues, with many claims targeting the student-led opposition list (fabricated positions on Kosovo, national identity and history), attempts to misrepresent its programme and candidates, and deceptive social-media graphics that could have led governing-party supporters to cast invalid ballots ahead of the 25 October vote. MEDDMO reports that TikTok removed a network of nine fake 'news' accounts - 433 followers, Malta-based, engaged in coordinated inauthentic behaviour - that amplified narratives favourable to Malta's Labour Party, with TikTok finding no connection to the party. GLOBAL PULSE: IBERIFIER/Chequeado on Brazil's 25 October runoff between Lula da Silva and Flavio Bolsonaro after neither won outright on 4 October, where AI-generated deepfakes fabricated endorsements, fake voter testimonials and events that never occurred, drawing millions of views despite new rules requiring labelling of AI material. HDMO debunks a fabricated story that Ukraine's NABU found $140 million in cash in a raid linked to former defence procurement official Arsen Zhumadilov, spread via a website impersonating New Voice of Ukraine and a fake raid video (NABU said no such operation took place), reinforcing narratives that undermine trust in Ukraine. EDMO IRELAND/The Journal debunks an AI-altered video presented as a BBC News report claiming Indian and Israeli intelligence orchestrated the attempted Flydubai Dubai-Tel Aviv crash as a 'false flag' to implicate Iran; investigators say the plane made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia after the co-pilot attacked the captain. DE FACTO fact-checks six claims about the EU 'Chat Control' extension: the vote extended until 2028 a 2021 rule letting services voluntarily scan for known child-abuse content (separate from the still-unadopted mandatory-scanning CSAR proposal) - contrary to viral claims it does not introduce mass surveillance, does not affect encrypted apps like WhatsApp or Signal and does not end encryption; the '80% of arrests' claim misquotes a Commission report that referred to investigations opened, not arrests; claims of unanimous expert opposition are misleading (800+ cryptographers criticised the future CSAR proposal, not July's vote, while some child-protection groups and Europol support scanning); and the text passed despite more MEPs voting against it (314-276) because extending existing legislation requires an absolute majority to reject it. IBERIFIER on the Falklands/Malvinas sovereignty debate revived by the Argentina-England World Cup semi-final. ZOOM-IN also carries TjekDet's 'AI & You' item: the Enigma Museum in Denmark launched a nationwide school course on the history and societal role of artificial intelligence. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: NewsGuard/IDMO - fake articles attributed to seemingly trustworthy outlets rose from 44 in the first half of 2025 to 421 in the first half of 2026, an 850% increase, with 163 outlets imitated, from the BBC, Politico, Bloomberg News and Euronews to The Kyiv Independent, The Yorkshire Post and La Montagne.
