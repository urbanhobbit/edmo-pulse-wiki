## War in Ukraine: AI-powered disinformation targets mobilization

**October 1, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

War in Ukraine: AI-powered disinformation targets mobilization. Disinformation about the war in Ukraine has been a constant phenomenon since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022 and, according to EDMO monitoring, has consistently been the main target of disinformation in the European Union in recent months, alongside migration. Recurrent Russian-driven narratives: the Ukrainian government, military and population are largely pro-Nazi; Ukrainian refugees are violent or parasitic and European countries treat them better than their own citizens; Zelenskyy and his entourage are drug users and corrupt; the war is NATO's fault; and it is the West that is seeking escalation. War propaganda is as old as war and used by both sides, but a significant development over the past two years is the growing use of AI to create short videos - usually 5 to 15 seconds long - that are realistic enough to appear credible even though often not technically deepfakes, depicting precisely what propaganda would like to be true: EDMO had already detected a significant wave in late 2025 tied to the Russian offensive on Pokrovsk, with numerous AI-generated videos spread by pro-Russian accounts showing Ukrainian soldiers surrendering or crying. In September a new wave of pro-Russian AI-generated videos was detected, this time focusing on the mobilization of Ukrainian soldiers to the front - soldiers in handcuffs on buses being forcibly taken away - happening alongside Ukrainian armed-forces successes in the Donbas (the liberation of several towns north of Lyman as part of Operation Vivaldi) and intensifying reports of a possible new mobilisation in Russia. The message can target several audiences (the Russian public, the Ukrainian public and public opinion in European countries allied with Kyiv), and the videos are being recirculated in several European countries with subtitles in different languages; AI-generated videos and images are not the only tools, as the campaign also uses the 'old' technique of taking real videos or images and distorting their meaning with false captions - for instance videos and images of a protest against the cutting down of trees in Kyiv circulated with the false claim that they showed Ukrainian civilians being taken away by soldiers for trying to disrupt a recruitment operation. The most concerning element is the relatively low quality and limited coordination of these operations, which combine generative AI with other techniques: the potential for technical and tactical improvement is enormous, and if this content is already capable of causing harm by being believed by more vulnerable segments of the public, thinking about what it could do in the future is chilling. ZOOM-IN (the war increasingly fought in the digital information space, where AI-enabled tools amplify deceptive narratives, manipulate images and facilitate targeted recruitment): NORDIS/TjekDet citing an NGO Code for Africa report exposed 33 recruitment groups running nearly 300 websites and social-media accounts that use deceptive ads - mainly on Facebook and often featuring AI-generated images - to recruit young men from Africa, the Middle East, Asia and Latin America for Russia's war in Ukraine, promising lucrative jobs as security guards, actors or drivers in Russia with monthly salaries exceeding $3,000; upon arrival the men are pressured into signing Russian-language military contracts and sent to the frontlines, many face confiscated passports, threats or unpaid bonuses, and one in five identified African recruits has been killed; experts from the Danish Institute for International Studies note the foreign-recruitment surge offsets domestic soldier shortages while shielding Russia's middle class from mobilization. Ironically the campaigns rely heavily on Western infrastructure: the recruitment group 'IRC Russian Bear' hosts and distributes its campaign materials from a server physically located in Amsterdam; multiple recruitment landing pages and persuasive campaign graphics are built with AI generation tools from US and Swedish companies; and applicant data (private contact details, background profiles and passport information) is collected using software owned by an Austrian tech company before being forwarded to operators in Russia. FACT debunks claims that Kyiv is experiencing a mass exodus as Russia bombards the city: social-media posts circulated photos and videos allegedly showing residents fleeing, but fact-checkers found much of the material misleading or unrelated - one video showed ordinary rush-hour activity in a Kyiv metro station, an image supposedly depicting a traffic jam leaving Kyiv was actually taken at a toll station in China, another photograph showed Ukrainians moving toward the Polish border in 2022 rather than residents fleeing Kyiv in 2026, and a genuine video of Kyiv traffic was misrepresented as evidence of people escaping; analysts say such narratives exaggerate real hardships to create panic, undermine morale and potentially weaken international support for Ukraine, and there is no public evidence of a current mass departure. FACT on AI-generated assessments used to amplify a false claim that a maritime drone destroyed near Romania's Neptun Deep gas platform was 'undoubtedly' Ukrainian: several Romanian media outlets, Facebook pages and Telegram channels cited supposed analyses by ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok as authoritative evidence (one article received over 1,000 reactions, 240 comments and 300 shares, another more than 3,000 reactions, 700 comments and 800 shares), but Fact Hub's experiment found that when given identical photographs and neutral questions the three models produced substantially different identifications, ranging from a possible British-derived vessel to a likely Russian-origin drone and a Ukrainian MAGURA V5, demonstrating how AI outputs can acquire credibility and resonance on social media despite uncertainty, contradictory evidence and the models' own acknowledged limitations. ELECTION BEAT: NORDIS/Kallkritikbyran on Sweden's election aftermath - misinformation around voter fraud, electoral interference and AI-generated content, with minor voting irregularities and discarded ballots often presented as evidence of deliberate fraud and AI-generated images including a fabricated story showing ballots dumped into a river; researchers also identified conspiracy theories alleging manipulation by 'globalist elites' and portraying immigrants as being used to influence the vote (such as an AI-generated Islamophobic picture), narratives that continued circulating after election day; while foreign actors were active in amplifying misleading content, researchers found no immediate evidence of large-scale coordinated foreign interference or significant impact on voters, with much of the misinformation appearing to originate domestically - highlighting how existing political narratives, conspiracy theories and readily available AI tools can contribute to information disorder around elections. GADMO/AFP on postal voting as a target of a disinformation campaign in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern: a staged video circulated during Berlin's September 2026 parliamentary election falsely claiming to show postal ballots for the AfD being deliberately destroyed while CDU votes were preserved, fuelling accusations of election fraud, but AFP found the envelopes and documents shown differed from official Berlin postal voting materials and the Berlin state election authority confirmed the video was fake, highlighting inconsistencies with official vote-counting procedures; the video was part of a wider pattern of false claims about postal voting during German state elections in autumn 2026, with similar content in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Saxony-Anhalt, some posts attracting unusually high numbers of views compared with their engagement (suggesting possible artificial amplification), and several accounts spreading the claims linked to pro-Russian disinformation networks previously associated with campaigns targeting European elections and promoting narratives favourable to Russia and the AfD. EDMO on Serbia: President Aleksandar Vucic announced extraordinary parliamentary elections for 25 October 2026 despite previously linking the timing of an early vote to the international security situation - in April, amid calls from protesting students for early elections, he said there would be no summer vote if a major global crisis persisted, suggesting elections could instead take place if progress towards peace was made; over the following months he continued to warn about the economic and security consequences of the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, and in early September expressed concern about growing international tensions and potential wider conflicts while predicting the situation would worsen during the winter; nevertheless on 9 September, following a proposal from the Serbian government, he dissolved parliament and called an early election, and Serbian fact-checking outlet Istinomer assessed his position as inconsistent with his earlier statements. GLOBAL PULSE: CEDMO/Demagog.pl on who creates climate disinformation - it emerges from the intersection of business interests, political actors, propaganda and influence operations, and contemporary climate disinformation rarely denies climate change outright but more often presents climate policies as ineffective, unrealistic, unfair or economically damaging; the article identifies fossil-fuel interests, political actors, think tanks, media, influencers, bots and trolls as participants, and highlights documented historical cases involving Exxon and Shell, whose internal documents acknowledged climate risks while public communications emphasised scientific uncertainty. It also examines Russia's role, arguing climate-related narratives can serve Moscow's economic and geopolitical interests by weakening support for decarbonisation and exploiting divisions over energy prices, renewables and EU policies; many narratives arise locally with local economic or political roots and Russia often does not need to invent conflicts but simply seeks out existing tensions and amplifies them on social media, as seen after the tragic flooding in Valencia when Russian messages reinforced existing criticism of the Spanish authorities and linked the country's weakness to support for Ukraine. DE FACTO/Les Surligneurs examines claims that solar panels significantly increase temperatures and therefore undermine their climate benefits, finding that photovoltaic installations can indeed alter local temperatures but the effect varies considerably by location, weather, panel efficiency and what surfaces they replace: studies recorded increases of up to 5.2 degrees C near rooftop panels during extreme heat, while other research found nighttime warming of 3-4 degrees C above ground-based solar farms; conversely panels can sometimes reduce temperatures, particularly when replacing heat-absorbing surfaces such as asphalt. The article stresses that these localized effects should not be confused with global climate impact - over their life cycle solar panels produce substantially fewer greenhouse-gas emissions than fossil-fuel electricity, and the available evidence indicates photovoltaic energy has a considerably lower carbon footprint than fossil fuels. GADMO on a social-media graphic claiming Germany gives China 630 million euros in annual development aid while China builds hundreds of coal-fired power plants and plans hundreds more: the claim is misleading because it combines outdated figures with current political debates - the figure dates from 2017 and referred largely to repayable loans, not conventional development aid, and Germany has since ended its classic bilateral development cooperation with China, with current German-funded projects much smaller (around 19 million euros committed to environmental and social initiatives, not paid directly to the Chinese government); the figures of about 368 coal plants under construction and 803 planned are also around a decade old, although China continues to expand coal power (94.5 GW of new coal projects began construction in 2024) while rapidly expanding solar energy, which by July 2026 had slightly exceeded coal in installed capacity. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: the International Panel on the Information Environment's 'Trends in the Information Environment: 2026 Expert Survey Results' - lack of platform accountability was the most widely shared concern, rated a big or extreme threat by 78% of experts, narrowly ahead of misinformation at 73% and polarization at 69%, followed by the dominance of a few social media platforms at 64% and filter bubbles or echo chambers at 53%; generative AI was rated the smallest of the six threats, though still a big or extreme threat for 47% of experts. On barriers, availability of research funding was the most common at 67%, ahead of data access at 60% (funding overtook data access as the top barrier in 2025 and remained the top barrier this year), while pressure to align research with a government's agenda rose from 21% to 33%, government censorship from 11% to 23%, and pressure from private companies from 8% to 18%.
