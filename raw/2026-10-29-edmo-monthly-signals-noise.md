## The Factory of Discord: How Foreign Actors Exploit Social Unrest in Europe

**October 29, 2026** — EDMO *Signals & Noise*, monthly overview of emerging disinformation trends
Author: **Paolo Cesarini**, Chair of the Executive Board, EDMO

### Overview

Street protests erupted in several European countries in recent weeks — France (crumbling public schools, educational inequality), Italy (17–18 October riots against living costs), Spain, Belgium, Portugal and Greece — mixing legitimate grievances (living costs, low salaries, job insecurity, social housing, education funding, rearmament costs, the war in Ukraine) with social-media manipulation and unjustifiable violence. Disinformation networks exploit this discontent to fuel polarisation, xenophobia and mistrust in EU institutions by collapsing distinct grievances into one narrative: **a Europe in decline**, unable to secure itself against the "Islamisation" of its societies and unable to defend its citizens' interests.

### European protests: a Russian plot?

Government officials and international media floated the hypothesis of a single mastermind (the Kremlin, Iran, Tunisia) behind the simultaneous unrest. EDMO's verdict is explicit: there is **documented evidence that foreign actors exploited the tensions, but no substantiated claim of a single command**.

- The *Financial Times* reported European authorities examining possible Russian disinformation linked to the French unrest: pro-Russian and far-right accounts reposted images of violence, portraying France as a country in chaos. Networks named: **Storm-1516** and **Matryoshka**. The same report noted no concrete evidence of Russian orchestration of the demonstrations.
- An alternative attribution — "out-of-control mass migration" and Islam — was pushed by Donald Trump and Republican figures and dismissed by President Macron as "foolish".
- **Facta** (23 October) confirmed available evidence does not corroborate attributing the Paris, Milan, Turin and Bologna protests to Russian or other foreign actors. Political statements and opened investigations are not an assessment of responsibility.
- Yet evidence keeps mounting of a well-organised, multilayered Kremlin-run influence infrastructure in Europe: YouTube influencer recruitment in Estonia, Russian cyberattacks in the Nordic countries, bogus think tanks (EDMO Weekly Pulse, 15 October).
- The observed activity is **predominantly reactive**: propaganda exploits events already underway rather than triggering them. Example: a video viewed millions of times of protesters throwing incendiary devices, presented as France, actually shows **Tbilisi, Georgia, 2024** — authentic footage, wrong place and time.

### Ukraine, Zelensky and the war of responsibility

Delegitimising Zelensky and Western support for Ukraine remains a core objective of pro-Russian disinformation. AI-generated videos purporting to show Ukrainian citizens dragged onto buses for the front circulate as "real people" testimonies, feeding the image of a brutal state and undermining confidence in mobilisation. A second example: Trump's misrepresentation of the causes of fuel price increases before announcing a "diesel deal" with Putin (EDMO Weekly Pulse, 22 October) — omitting the US/Israel war against Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Different foreign actors converge on the same target: blaming Kyiv for the war's economic costs and recasting support for Ukraine as a sunk cost imposed on Western citizens.

### From social protests to ethnic war

A second strand converts protests into anti-immigration arguments. The October EDMO Monthly Situational Report describes the French riots being inserted into a broader "European collapse" narrative blaming African, Muslim and Black immigrants: saturating the online space with looting and arson footage, passing off old or decontextualised footage as new anti-immigration demonstrations, and attributing individuals' actions to entire communities. Amplifiers include far-right influencers, European ultranationalists, MAGA activists and Trump himself — e.g. US streamer **Asmongold (Zack Hoyt)** describing Italy as on the verge of civil war (~1.7 million YouTube views, per Facta). Convergence of foreign propaganda and xenophobic rhetoric does not prove a common command — but **interests can coincide**: the Kremlin gains from weakening European cohesion, nativist movements from fear of migrants. The "remigration" rhetoric is the most radical expression, recoding social problems as ethnic-religious conflict.

### The role of digital platforms

Platforms acted as massive amplifiers of organic mobilisation and violent copycat behaviour. Young demonstrators used TikTok, Instagram and X to document clashes; Italian students in Rome and Milan adopted the slogan "Let's do as in France". Rather than one coordinating mastermind, highly visual, fast, non-moderated content produced a **contagion effect**, with recommender systems pushing high-engagement chaotic footage to similar demographics across Europe — turning local student grievances into a continental "aesthetic of unrest". Russian officials (Alexei Pushkov, Maria Zakharova) framed events as "civil war" or a "failing EU"; OSINT analyses show this framing promoted via state media to Russian audiences and targeted at Western audiences by Storm-1516 across X, Telegram and TikTok. Caution is warranted: in EDMO's analysis of the Ukrainian strikes on Yandex data centres, a graph showing a 56% drop in AfD-linked bot traffic turned out to be **AI-generated**, and other analyses found no significant change — a chart alone never proves a coordinated operation.

### Who benefits from the discord?

- **Russia-aligned influence networks**: hybrid attacks to undermine confidence in the EU and divide over support to Kyiv.
- **Far-right movements**: using unrest to promote anti-immigration and identity narratives.
- **Influencers and content creators**: visibility and revenue from extreme content.
- **The Trump administration**, in sync with European far-right ideologues, fuelling polarisation in the name of a putative "European Greatness".

### Three conclusions

1. **Insufficient evidence** to attribute the protests in France, Italy or elsewhere to a single Russian, Islamist or other source.
2. **Foreign influence operations are a real risk** — pro-Russian networks are actively exploiting European social tensions through an increasingly sophisticated infrastructure, and other foreign actors may rally on the same grievances.
3. **Domestic far-right movements are capitalising** on the interferences to turn social problems into identity conflicts.

Ahead of elections in Spain and in Serbia's pre-election dynamics, the risk is that disparate problems collapse into one catastrophic explanation — "Europe is collapsing, institutions have lost control, conflicts are irreconcilable" — eroding trust in the democratic model even without convincing everyone of the same theories.

### Democratic response

Avoid two symmetric errors: downplaying foreign interference, and attributing every manifestation of dissent to an external power. What is needed: rigorous verification, transparency of amplification networks, effective platform accountability — and above all, addressing the real causes of the protests (cost of living, job insecurity, quality of education, uncertainty about the future). Ignoring them leaves fertile ground for both foreign and domestic manipulation.
