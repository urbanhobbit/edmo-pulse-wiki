## Sections

### ON THE RISE
So much the worse for the facts?

One of the most remarkable features of disinformation is its ability to maintain the appearance of being right even when reality proves otherwise. Russian disinformation routinely fabricates (false) evidence of NATO troops being present in Ukraine because the facts did not fit the narrative. US disinformation continues to repeat the claim that the EU censors free speech, despite the State Department finding no evidence to support that claim.

The ongoing FIFA World Cup in the United States — particularly the match between France and Morocco — generated waves of racist and Islamophobic disinformation. Videos showing riots and violence in Paris circulated with misleading captions falsely linking them to France's victory. No such unrest had taken place; the videos were old footage from unrelated events.

Confirmation bias and cognitive laziness make us particularly vulnerable to disinformation, and can even turn us into its unconscious amplifiers.

### ZOOM-IN
- **Racism is the main form of abuse on social media during the World Cup according to FIFA** (IBERIFIER): FIFA's Social Media Protection Service monitored 6M+ posts during the group stage, with 89,000 identified as abusive. 11% contained racist content, up from 8% in 2022. 100 legally attributable cases identified.
- **Algeria-Austria match supposedly "fixed": why these unfounded accusations?** (DE FACTO): Examines unfounded allegations that the 3–3 draw between Algeria and Austria was fixed, based on manipulated odds and social media speculation with no factual basis.
- **Infantino did not say that the president of Mexico offered him money to win the World Cup: the video was created with AI** (VERIFIERS): An AI-generated video falsely shows FIFA President Gianni Infantino claiming that the President of Mexico offered a bribe to ensure Mexico wins the World Cup.

### GLOBAL PULSE
- **AI funeral images inflate support for Iran's former Supreme Leader**: AI-generated images of mass mourning for Iran's former Supreme Leader circulate online to artificially inflate perceptions of public support.
- **US President shares old, AI-manipulated photo as evidence of new US strike on Iran**: An old, AI-manipulated photo is shared as evidence of a new US strike on Iran, with a warning it can "get much worse."
- **Anti-NATO disinformation surged around Ankara summit**: Disinformation narratives targeting NATO intensified around the Ankara summit, pushing claims about NATO expansion and aggression.
- **Wildfire conspiracy theories target renewable energy projects**: Conspiracy theories falsely link wildfires to renewable energy installations.
