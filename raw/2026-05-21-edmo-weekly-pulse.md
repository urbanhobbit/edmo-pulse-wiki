## Overview

This edition examines how crime stories involving immigrants are weaponised by far-right extremists and clickbait merchants to spread racism, xenophobia and Islamophobia across social media. The ZOOM-IN section examines three cases of anti-migrant disinformation across Spain, Ireland, and Hungary/Austria. ELECTION BEAT covers electoral disinformation in Cyprus, Malta, and Andalusia. GLOBAL PULSE examines fake war images, AI-generated political influencers for Trump, and anti-migrant narratives targeting Indian communities in Ukraine. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE reports on the failure of major AI image detection tools.

## ON THE RISE — Crime, migrants and disinformation: an online banquet for extremists, a real-world danger for societies

It is well known that far-right extremism on social media, along with the clickbait tactics of those seeking to monetize engagement, thrives on crime stories involving immigrants and foreign nationals. When the individuals involved are Muslim, the specter of terrorism is immediately invoked. And if the facts fail to fit that narrative — for example, if the perpetrator is not an immigrant, or not Muslim — so much the worse for the facts. False content will still circulate across social media platforms, including false news, AI-generated videos and images, or material taken from unrelated contexts. Even genuine reports are accompanied by manipulative commentary aimed at stigmatizing the usual targets.

Key cases: The tragedy in Modena, Italy (Italian citizen of Moroccan origin with mental illness drove into crowd — mainstream media circulated wrong photo); Leipzig car attack (German citizen, but far-right falsely claimed Afghan/Syrian refugee); Southport, UK (2024, three girls killed by British-born Rwandan-descent boy — sparked far-right riots); Dublin (similar pattern). Pro-Russian networks also use this schema against Ukrainian refugees in Eastern Europe.

## ZOOM-IN

Street crimes, crowd killings and other acts of violence continue to feed false, misleading or simplified narratives targeting migrants and steering racism and xenophobia. Each case becomes a pretext for pushing populist political agendas, including targeting civil society organisations, charities, and EU institutions. The incidents fit into the "great replacement" conspiracy theory.

**Spanish woman euthanized due to terminal injury: Claims she was raped by migrants are unfounded** (CEDMO) — Noelia Castillo underwent euthanasia after irreversible spinal injuries from a suicide attempt. Viral false claims alleged she was raped by migrant minors. Vox leader Santiago Abascal amplified the falsehood on X.

**NGOs and charities targeted by "bait emails" asking about how to illegally enter Ireland** (EDMO IRELAND) — 85% of surveyed organisations said misinformation makes it harder to achieve their goals. Staff reported racist and misogynistic abuse.

**Hungarian and Austrian news outlets claim the EU would label any criticism of its migration policy as "institutional racism"** (HDMO/Lakmusz) — Magyar Nemzet falsely claimed the EU's 2026-2030 anti-racism strategy would introduce "re-education programs" and punish migration criticism. None of these claims appear in the actual European Commission document.

## ELECTION BEAT

**Elections in Cyprus and Malta: Findings and Monitoring From MedDMO** — AI-generated "slopaganda" rising in Malta's general elections. Net News (Nationalist Party Facebook page) taken down after coordinated mass-reporting campaign. Political ads continue despite Meta/Google bans.

**Alleged interference in the vote and in the electoral campaign: the hoaxes of the Andalusian elections** (IBERIFIER) — Andalusian elections held 17 May. Viral video falsely showed Adelante Andalucía representative forcing elderly woman to change vote (footage from 2023 Granada elections). False claims about singer Miguel Ríos losing ticket sales after endorsing PSOE.

## GLOBAL PULSE

**Manipulating Public Perceptions of Conflict: A Deep Dive Into Fake Images of War** (BENEDMO) — Misleading war images circulate widely: recycled video game footage, AI-generated scenes, photographs from past conflicts. Visual content spreads faster than textual misinformation. Advances in digital tools lower barriers to producing convincing false content.

**Posing as Real Users, AI Generated "Influencers" Campaign for Trump Ahead of US Midterms** (NORDIS/TjekDet) — Network of AI-generated personas promoting Trump content, creating illusion of grassroots support. Unlike traditional disinformation relying on false claims, these operations work through amplification and perception manipulation.

**Global Communities Exploited to Amplify Anti Migration Disinformation: The Narrative of Indian Migrants in Ukraine** (BECID/Delfi) — Viral video allegedly showing Indian migrants washing clothes in Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine was misattributed and repurposed from original context to suggest cultural incompatibility.

## ON A DIFFERENT NOTE

**Major AI Image Detection Tools Mislead Online Users, Often Classifying Authentic Images as Fake** (NewsGuard audit published by IDMO) — Major AI image detection tools frequently misclassify authentic images as fake, undermining trust in digital verification.
