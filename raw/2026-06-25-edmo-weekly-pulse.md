## Overview

The 25 June 2026 edition of the EDMO Weekly Pulse focuses on the explosion of AI-generated hypersexualized content during the FIFA World Cup, platform monetization failures, and the darker dynamics where AI-generated images are used to lure users into far-right propaganda networks. The issue also covers Meta removing fake hockey fan pages during the Stanley Cup playoffs, election disinformation in Malta and Armenia, Russia's migrant disinformation campaign in Ukraine, data poisoning of AI models, and a landmark German court ruling holding Google liable for false AI summaries.

## ON THE RISE — The World Cup of Hypersexualized Fakes?

Disinformation generally follows information, particularly when a crisis or an important event happens. This is especially true for AI-generated content — images and videos not labeled as such — and the ongoing FIFA World Cup is the latest confirmation.

One category of AI-generated content has gone particularly viral: images of hyper-sexualized female supporters of national teams. Fact-checking organizations across Europe verified images from Belgium, Germany, Argentina, Switzerland, Mexico, Spain, Brazil, and more — all AI-generated. The images are created primarily for monetization: social media platforms' monetization programs reward viral posts with views, likes, and shares. Accounts are often linked to OnlyFans for further monetization of explicit content.

The consequences include blurring the lines between fiction and reality, negative impacts on young users (unrealistic expectations for boys, anxiety and depression for girls who don't match AI-generated beauty stereotypes), and darker dynamics where AI-generated images are used to attract users into far-right nationalist propaganda networks mixed with soft-core pornography.

FIFA World Cup disinformation also includes other categories: non-labeled AI-generated humorous images drawing clicks and shares, and conspiracy theories about specific players — notably a false claim about Kylian Mbappé leaving PSG because of alleged sexual harassment by French President Emmanuel Macron, published on a website mimicking Eurosport's and bearing hallmarks of a pro-Russian disinformation campaign.

## ZOOM-IN

### Meta removes fake hockey fan pages amid the 2026 Stanley Cup playoffs

From CEDMO. Meta removed 39 Facebook pages after an AFP investigation revealed they were spreading false content targeting hockey fans during the 2026 Stanley Cup playoffs. The pages, managed from Vietnam, exploited NHL interest to generate engagement and drive traffic to ad-heavy websites, amassing about 244,600 followers. Common tactics included fabricated "breaking news" about NHL players diagnosed with stage IV glioblastoma (falsely targeting Wayne Gretzky, Mario Lemieux, Mark Messier, Larry Robinson, and others) and unverified stories of large charitable donations.

### No, the United States did not close its border to travellers from Africa due to the threat of Ebola during the 2026 World Cup

From IBERIFIER. A viral TikTok claim that the U.S. closed its borders to African travellers during the 2026 World Cup due to Ebola is false. Instead, U.S. authorities introduced a temporary, limited health measure for individuals recently in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda, or South Sudan. Neither the U.S., Mexico, nor Canada closed borders to African travellers.

### No match assignment for referee Artan in Canada is known

From GADMO. Somali referee Omar Artan was denied entry to the U.S. in Miami due to alleged security concerns. A social media post by British Columbia politician David Eby welcoming Artan was interpreted as a World Cup refereeing assignment, but FIFA stated Artan would be unable to train or officiate after being refused U.S. entry. Somalia is on the U.S. travel ban list.

## ELECTION BEAT

### Fact-check Malta: Did PN leader Alex Borg win more votes than Robert Abela and all previous PN leaders?

From MEDDMO. Following Malta's May 31 election, PN supporters noted Alex Borg's 21,825 votes exceeded Robert Abela's 21,682 — technically accurate but incomplete. Borg ran in Gozo (Malta's largest district) inflating raw totals; by vote share, Abela led 39.6% to 37.5%. The "best-ever PN leader" claim falls apart: Simon Busuttil (2017) and Eddie Fenech Adami (2003) posted higher vote shares than Borg.

### Russia ramped up disinformation before Armenia vote

From ADMO. PM Nikol Pashinyan won a new term with ~49.8% of the vote on June 7. Researchers tracked 31 fake reports in one week accusing Pashinyan of election fraud and warmongering. One AI-generated video falsely claimed he had "aggressive cancer" using a cloned Euronews presenter's voice. Storm-1516 (linked to Russian intelligence) targeted Armenia more than any other country in the past year.

## GLOBAL PULSE

### Russia is flooding Ukraine with fake migrant stories — and it's working

From FACT. A coordinated pro-Russian campaign falsely suggests migrants are replacing Ukrainians, using AI-edited videos, recycled footage, and misleading clips. The campaign exploits fears around identity and the war to divide society and weaken trust.

### Data poisoning: the invisible threat targeting large AI models

From DE FACTO. As AI chatbots become central information sources, "data poisoning" — inserting misleading content into training data — is increasingly concerning. AI tools are attractive targets for influence operations since shaping training data can indirectly shape user views.

### Viral "Sex Slavery" Video Misidentified — Actually an Iraqi Performance

From IBERIFIER. A widely shared video purporting to show women sold as sexual slaves in Syria is actually a staged 2023 artistic performance by an Iraqi artist depicting Yazidi victims. Powerful visuals stripped of context turned awareness art into disinformation.

### No Evidence Unvaccinated Children Are "Healthier," Viral Claim Misleads

From ADMO. A viral claim that unvaccinated children are healthier is unsupported, originating from low-credibility disinformation-linked websites recycling the same content without reliable sources.

## ON A DIFFERENT NOTE

A German court ruled that Google's AI summary constitutes distinct content attributable to the search engine operator, holding Google liable for false information in AI answers. This landmark decision has significant implications for AI-generated content liability across the EU.
