## Even if it doesn't kill us all, AI can still make a lot of damage to our democracies

**September 25, 2026** — EDMO Weekly Pulse

Even if it does not kill us all, AI can still make a lot of damage to our democracies. The AI existential-risk debate moves from science fiction to the mainstream: the 9 September resignation of 27-year-old British AI researcher Jacob Coxon (previously OpenAI, then working on pretraining at Anthropic), who wrote that 'The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade', and the 12 September agreement by the CEOs of Anthropic (Dario Amodei), OpenAI (Sam Altman) and xAI (Elon Musk) that frontier AI development should be slowed with stronger safeguards and greater coordination. But the present is already deeply troubling: CNN reported on 18 September that an intelligence report almost prompted the US military to board a Chinese ship in the Middle East suspected of carrying nuclear-weapons components to Iran - the cargo was misidentified by an AI chatbot used by the analyst who drafted the report, and the mistake was caught only shortly before the planned operation, per a CNN source coming dangerously close to triggering an armed US-China conflict. The editorial argues human oversight is of the utmost importance because AI cannot be blindly trusted, and that in information integrity it is the constant drip of AI-generated falsehoods (amplified, discussed, made viral and monetised with the help of AI itself) that does the greatest damage - eroding trust in institutions, democracy and science, rewriting the past, spreading hate and panic, and reshaping perceptions around artificial realities that reward extremism and polarisation. A catastrophe is no less damaging simply because it unfolds in slow motion. ZOOM-IN: IBERIFIER/Chequeado on whether AI companies should slow development - Amodei, Altman and Musk support caution, external safety evaluations, common standards and coordination between governments, while Mark Zuckerberg and Palantir's Shyam Sankar oppose slowing progress over competition concerns; Chequeado distinguishes hypothetical future risks from harms already documented, citing the 2026 International AI Safety Report commissioned by the UK AI Security Institute (current systems do not yet have the capabilities to escape human control and cause catastrophic destruction but are developing relevant abilities; documented risks include disinformation, influence operations, fraud, cyberattacks and misuse for biological threats; Anthropic reported AI-generated fake identities used to conduct influence operations and Hugging Face reported an autonomous AI agent carrying out 17,600 actions during a cyberattack). IBERIFIER/Chequeado on AI-generated political personas: 'Lucia Libertaria' and 'Julia Riojana' promote pro-government content in Argentina without clearly disclosing their artificial nature, with similar practices in the US (a fictitious female soldier hosting pro-Trump messages), Poland (AI-generated young women promoting anti-EU and far-right narratives) and Germany (attractive synthetic influencers promoting the AfD); the latest evolution comes from Brazil's 4 October general election, where at least 50 AI-generated TikTok videos portraying fictitious voters - some praising, others criticising Lula and Flavio Bolsonaro - reached 7.4 million views overall, a shift from synthetic influencers to 'synthetic voters' that manufactures the appearance of spontaneous citizen testimony. GADMO/AFP on a supposed 1990s street survey video that is entirely AI-generated (95% probability of synthetic audio, 99% for image, unnatural movements, illegible shop signs, and anachronisms such as petrol at 30 pfennigs per litre though 1990s German prices were substantially higher); traced to an account called 'AI Satire' that originally published it with an AI label on TikTok, Facebook and Instagram, it circulated as genuine historical footage once detached from that context - AI reengineering the past to influence perceptions of the present. ELECTION BEAT: BECID/Re:Baltica on the Belarus-based propaganda channel 'Grani Svobodi' ('Borders of Freedom'), with almost 30,000 YouTube subscribers publishing multiple Russian-language videos daily, which uses defectors from Latvia and Lithuania to spread anti-Baltic and anti-Ukrainian narratives to Russian-speaking audiences (the Baltics 'collapsing' because of support for Ukraine, Russophobia and 'Nazism' accusations, questioning the legitimacy of Latvian elections, promoting closer ties with Russia and Belarus; many regular commentators are former activists, politicians and media figures who fled for Russia or Belarus and became active in pro-Kremlin or Belarusian state media); Re:Baltica also investigated Julija Stepanenko, leader and prime ministerial candidate of Latvia's populist right-wing 'Sovereign Power' (running with the 'Alliance of Young Latvia', focused on traditional family values, Christian-right politics and the Russian-speaking minority), who in mid-September 2026 refused to clarify whether she views Russia as a security threat, advocating 'pure diplomacy', while Latvia's State Security Service (SAB) and anti-corruption bureau (KNAB) investigated large financial gifts from her Russia-based father (no legal barrier to her candidacy was found). GADMO/AFP on AI-generated videos during the Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state election falsely claiming the AfD had been omitted from postal ballots in two constituencies: AFP compared the documents with official sample ballots and found the AfD listed in both, the state returning officer confirmed the ballots were forged, analysis tools indicated a 97-99% probability the audio was AI-generated, and an analyst from the Institute for Strategic Dialogue linked the content to the Russian disinformation campaign Storm-1516 (an account spreading the claim had previously amplified narratives associated with the campaign); similar false claims about missing or destroyed AfD ballots circulated around the 2025 federal election and the September 2026 Saxony-Anhalt state election. NORDIS/Kallkritikbyran's ex-post examination of Sweden's elections: misinformation around voter fraud, electoral interference and AI-generated content, with minor voting irregularities and discarded ballots presented as evidence of deliberate fraud and AI-generated images including a fabricated story showing ballots dumped into a river; conspiracy theories alleged manipulation by 'globalist elites' and portrayed immigrants as used to influence the vote (including an AI-generated Islamophobic picture); while foreign actors amplified misleading content, researchers found no immediate evidence of large-scale coordinated foreign interference or significant impact on voters - much of the misinformation originated domestically, highlighting how existing political narratives, conspiracy theories and readily available AI tools contribute to information disorder. MEDDMO on TikTok removing a network of nine fake 'news' accounts that sought to artificially amplify narratives favourable to Malta's Labour Party during the 2026 general election campaign: the Malta-based accounts targeted local users and engaged in coordinated inauthentic behaviour, presenting themselves as news sources, with 433 followers when removed; TikTok did not identify the operators or establish any connection with the Labour Party, which said the accounts were not among its official channels, and earlier reporting had identified TikTok accounts using AI-generated images and videos to attack political figures while a MEDDMO investigation documented websites created ahead of the election that posed as news or fact-checking outlets and promoted pro-Labour narratives. GLOBAL PULSE: IDMO/Facta on Western influencers living in Dubai who post reassuring videos about life in the UAE despite ongoing Iranian missile and drone attacks linked to the wider Middle East conflict, presenting Dubai as safe and well protected by its air defences (some claiming it is safer than many European cities) while other expatriates leave or seek repatriation; Facta notes the strikingly similar messaging mirroring official government communications, the UAE's heavy restrictions on criticism of the government and limited media freedom, and experts' questions about the long-term sustainability of the air-defence system. BROD/Factual fact-checks a viral claim that Europe will stop receiving Russian gas before the winter of 2026-2027 and therefore face widespread heating shortages: the claim misrepresents earlier comments by Vladimir Putin and ignores the EU's gradual phase-out of Russian gas (long-term pipeline contracts can remain in force until autumn 2027), and although EU gas storage levels are lower than in previous years, authorities see no immediate threat to gas security thanks to diversified suppliers, expanded LNG import capacity and reduced consumption, with higher prices and market volatility the main concern rather than households being left without heating. CEDMO on a network of AI-generated deepfake videos falsely depicting celebrities (Alyson Hannigan, Sarah Jessica Parker, Julia Roberts) attacking Democrats, incorporating fake CNN branding, circulating ahead of the 2026 US midterm elections; the celebrities have publicly rejected the fabricated statements, and researchers attribute the campaign to the Russian influence operation Matryoshka (also known as Operation Overload), previously linked to election-related disinformation in several European countries - an early sign of Russian efforts to influence the US midterms through manipulated videos, impersonated media brands and coordinated amplification. ON A DIFFERENT NOTE: the International AI Safety Report 2026 - 'loss of control' scenarios, in which AI systems operate outside anyone's control with no clear path to regaining it, are not possible with current systems but they are improving in relevant areas such as autonomous operation; since the last Report it has become more common for models to distinguish between test settings and real-world deployment and to find loopholes in evaluations, which could allow dangerous capabilities to go undetected before deployment.
