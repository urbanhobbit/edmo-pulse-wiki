## Overview

The 18 June 2026 edition examines the recurring pattern of far-right street violence across the UK and Ireland — from Dublin (2023) to Southport (2024) to Belfast (2026) — tracing the role of social media amplification, domestic far-right actors, foreign interference (MAGA ecosystem, Russian disinformation), and engagement-driven algorithms. The Zoom-In section covers three cases of anti-immigrant disinformation in Latvia, France, and Poland. Election Beat tracks Russian influence operations targeting Armenia's elections and Latvia's pre-election fact-checking preparations. Global Pulse covers Iran disinformation, rising threats against politicians on Facebook, and Argentina's tech deregulation plans. On a Different Note investigates pro-Russian hacker operations in the Nordics.

## ON THE RISE — Dublin, Southport, Belfast: Who Is Fanning the Flames?

Weekly Watch of Emerging Disinformation Risks

After Dublin in November 2023, Southport and other English cities in August 2024, Belfast has now become the latest flashpoint of far right unrest and street violence. The pattern is by now familiar: following a horrific crime in which the alleged perpetrator is a person of colour, social media channels associated with the far right quickly amplify the story. They often employ incendiary rhetoric and circulate distorted or outright false claims about the suspect's identity, frequently portraying them as an undocumented migrant or a Muslim, even when that is not the case. Calls for protests follow, crowds gather, and what begins as demonstrations against immigration or "crime" frequently descends into vandalism, rioting, and indiscriminate attacks on ethnic and religious minorities. The wishes of victims' families — who often call for restraint and an end to further violence — are largely ignored.

Those involved are often convinced they are defending their country from "ethnic replacement", Islamic extremism, or a perceived migrant "invasion". The belief that "civil war" in the UK, as Elon Musk wrote after the facts of Southport, "is inevitable" is widely shared online. Yet crime has generally declined over the same period in which the foreign-born population has increased significantly since the mid-1990s.

The role of social media platforms in shaping perceptions of reality is key — amplifying emotionally charged, violent and polarising content through engagement-driven algorithms. A range of actors pour fuel on volatility: domestic far right figures like Tommy Robinson, Conor McGregor and Nigel Farage; the MAGA ecosystem in the United States intervening in UK/Irish political debates; Elon Musk and his following amplifying disinformation; and Russian disinformation exploiting internal divisions (Tommy Robinson travelled to Moscow in June). Added to this, "influencers" like Geeth Sooriyapura ("king of Facebook advertising") have built extensive networks of Facebook groups reaching millions with xenophobic and racist content for monetisation.

## ZOOM-IN — A Closer Look at Cases Detected by the EDMO Network

**Third-Country Nationals Falsely Portrayed as Criminals in Latvia**
From BECID. A claim that Latvia would be seeing criminal groups composed of third-country nationals taking over drug distribution networks was pushed via NRA.lv by Austri Stankevics (National Alliance party youth affiliate). Amplified by local far-right politicians, Re:Baltica found the claim false — Latvia's State Police explicitly denied having any information indicating such groups are forming. Previous fact-checks found third-country nationals are suspected of crimes less frequently than Latvian citizens.

**The Image of a Street in Paris Destroyed "After the Passage of Hordes of Africans" Was Made With AI**
From IBERIFIER. Viral messages on social media claimed an image showed a destroyed street in Paris "after hordes of Africans have passed through." According to Newtral's analysis, the image was AI-generated — Hive AI assessed 99.9% probability. The image was also shared by a far-right Spanish MEP. Separately, footage of a gas pipeline explosion in Dagestan, Russia was manipulated with a soundtrack of "send them home" chants to falsely link it to Belfast unrest.

**Disinformation About the Krakow Integration Centre Fits Into a Broader Xenophobic Narrative**
From CEDMO. A claim circulated by Austri Stankevics (same Latvia source) alleged criminal groups of third-country nationals in Latvia. Found false by Re:Baltica. The article argues the claim fits a broader pattern of exaggerated narratives linking immigrants to crime.

## ELECTION BEAT — Tracking electoral disinformation through EDMO Hubs

**A Turkish Propagandist Fueling Russian Disinformation Ahead of Armenia's Elections**
From IDMO. Turkish self-described journalist Okay Deprem was identified as the source of roughly 40% of false claims in Storm-1516, one of Russia's most extensive influence operations targeting Armenia. NewsGuard traced 17 of 43 fabricated narratives against Armenian PM Nikol Pashinyan directly to Deprem, including allegations of corruption, family scandals, and constitutional manipulation. Deprem publishes through real Turkish outlets, lending his content credibility. Armenia is now Russia's top disinformation target, surpassing Ukraine.

**Before the Vote: Latvia's Fact-Checkers Gear Up as Misinformation Spreads**
From BECID. Latvia's parliamentary election campaign opened June 6. Re:Baltica outlined its coverage plan: fact-checking politicians' statements, monitoring social media, auditing MPs, and tracking foreign interference. Already, a viral Facebook video falsely claimed Latvians may be unable to vote with eID cards and that pensioners must pay €50 for a passport — both claims false.

## GLOBAL PULSE — Disinformation narratives shaping the world's conversations

**"Dream Country" Iran? Viral Claims Collapse Under Fact Check**
From BECID. Posts portraying Iran as a "dream country" with no taxes, no unemployment and generous payments for everyone are false. Delfi's investigation shows Iran does have taxes, unemployment is a real issue, and while social support exists, coverage is limited and uneven. The country faces economic hardship, inflation and widespread poverty.

**"More Speech and Fewer Mistakes"? Threats and Harassment Surge Against Politicians on Facebook**
From NORDIS. Hostile online behaviour toward politicians is rising sharply. Analysis of millions of comments found violent threats including calls for murder have quadrupled. The spike is linked to Meta rolling back key moderation rules, allowing more extreme content to spread.

**Argentina's "Silicon Valley" Plans Raise Questions on Oversight**
From IBERIFIER. Argentina's government is pushing tech reforms including AI-run companies and blockchain-based DAOs with minimal human oversight. Reduced oversight could make it harder to track who controls digital entities, manage data, or enforce accountability — creating potential loopholes.

## ON A DIFFERENT NOTE — How Pro-Russian Hackers Operate in The Nordics

An investigation by NORDIS Hub and partners. In 2026 alone, Denmark has been attacked 247 times by the hacker groups NORDIS has been following — by far the highest number among the Nordic countries. The investigation details how pro-Russian hackers operate in the Nordics.
